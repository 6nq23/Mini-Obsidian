# Technology & AI

> [!abstract]
> **Overview:** AI and technology are transforming every sector. Opportunities span: automation, prediction, personalization, content generation, and entirely new categories. Building AI businesses requires: access to data, computing power, talent, clear problem, and discipline on cost. Hype around AI creates bubbles; sober founders focus on solving specific problems with demonstrable ROI, not just "AI" positioning.

## What the Experts Say
This topic was covered by **2 guests** on the Figuring Out podcast: [[Aravind Srinivas]] (Perplexity AI) and [[Ajai Chowdhry]] (HCL Co-Founder).

## Key Themes & Insights

### Theme 1: AI as Tool, Not Destination
AI isn't the goal; solving customer problems is. Best AI companies: solve specific problems (faster processing, better predictions, personalization), demonstrate clear ROI, have access to data/compute. Startups adding "AI" to everything are speculative; focused AI companies solving specific problems create value. Perplexity built billion-dollar search alternative solving specific problem.

> [!tip]
> Focus on problem, not AI. AI is tool if it solves problem better/cheaper/faster than alternatives.

### Theme 2: Data is Moat
AI models require data. Companies with access to unique, proprietary data build defensibility. Data advantages: better models, faster learning, continuous improvement. Startups without data access: use open models, license data, or partner with data owners.

> [!tip]
> Data is more valuable than algorithms. Build to collect proprietary data. Data creates defensibility.

### Theme 3: Automation Creates New Categories
Technology enables automation of previously manual processes: customer support (chatbots), content creation (AI writing), image generation, code generation. These automations disrupt existing sectors while creating new opportunities. Be early in understanding automation impact on your industry. Ajai Chowdhry emphasizes technology's role in sector disruption.

> [!tip]
> Understand automation impact on your sector. Early movers in automation gain competitive advantage.

### Theme 4: Talent and Compute Costs Matter
Building sophisticated AI requires: ML engineers (expensive), GPUs/compute (expensive), data preparation (time-consuming). Cost-sensitive startups: use cloud APIs, open-source models, outsourced labeling. Building custom models requires significant capital. Aravind Srinivas built Perplexity starting with APIs then building specialized tech.

> [!tip]
> Start with APIs and open models. Custom models require significant capital and talent.

### Theme 5: Prediction Markets and Uncertainty
AI excels at prediction in well-defined domains (demand, churn, next purchase). Prediction ROI is clearer than automation. Companies profiting from better predictions: retailer forecasting demand, bank predicting churn, e-commerce personalizing recommendations.

> [!tip]
> Prediction has clearer ROI than automation. Build prediction models where mistakes have cost.

### Theme 6: Generative AI Disruption and Opportunities
LLMs (large language models) disrupted: content creation, coding assistance, customer support, knowledge work. This creates opportunities: better interfaces, specialized models, vertical applications. But also disrupts existing content, support, and coding jobs. Aravind Srinivas built Perplexity on top of LLM technology with specialized search interface.

> [!tip]
> Generative AI is disrupting knowledge work. Build specialized applications on top of LLMs.

### Theme 7: Responsible AI and Infrastructure
AI systems learn from biased training data, perpetuating human biases. Responsible AI requires: understanding bias, testing fairness, being transparent about limitations. Companies ignoring bias risk: regulatory backlash, customer loss, ethical issues. IT sector evolution (per Ajai Chowdhry) emphasizes responsible deployment.

> [!tip]
> Test AI for bias. Be transparent about limitations. Responsible AI builds long-term trust.

## Consolidated ✅ Practical Action Steps
> [!tip]
> Actions distilled from all guests.

1. **Identify AI Problem, Not AI Solution**: What specific problem? How does AI solve it better/faster/cheaper?

2. **Evaluate Data Requirements**: What data needed? Can you access it? Is collecting expensive?

3. **Start with APIs**: Use OpenAI, cloud ML APIs. Develop product. Optimize/build custom model if capital available.

4. **Measure ROI Clearly**: AI should reduce costs, improve efficiency, or increase revenue. Quantify benefit before building.

5. **Understand Bias and Fairness**: Test AI output for bias. Be transparent about limitations. Responsible AI builds trust.

6. **Build Specialized Applications**: Don't build LLMs; build applications on top of them. Specialize in domain.

7. **Track Talent and Compute Costs**: Budget for expensive resources. Outsource if cheaper. Focus capital on differentiation.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways Across All Episodes
> [!note]
> Core lessons.

- **Problem, Not AI**: Focus on solving customer problems. AI is tool, not destination.
- **Data is Defensibility**: Proprietary data creates moat. AI without data is commodity.
- **Automation Disrupts and Creates**: Automation displaces jobs; creates new opportunities. Be early.
- **Prediction Has Clear ROI**: Prediction value clearer than automation. Invest where mistakes costly.
- **Generative AI Disrupting Knowledge Work**: LLMs disrupt coding, writing, support. Build specialized applications on top.
- **Talent and Compute Expensive**: Start with APIs, not building. Custom models require significant capital.
- **Responsible AI Builds Trust**: Test for bias. Transparency about limitations. Responsible AI long-term advantage.

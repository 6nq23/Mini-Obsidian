# Fundraising & VC

> [!abstract]
> **Overview:** Fundraising is enabling but not determinative for startup success. VCs fund teams with product-market fit, strong founders, and defensible ideas. The best approach combines raising capital with maintaining unit economics discipline. Post-fundraising, execution, metrics, and customer satisfaction determine outcomes. VCs and founders have aligned interests on exit, not always aligned on company building.

## What the Experts Say
Key insights from VCs and founders on fundraising strategy.

## Key Themes & Insights

### Theme 1: Fundraising is Enabling, Not Determinative
Many founders obsess over raising funds, assuming capital solves problems. Capital solves one problem: time. More runway means more time to find product-market fit. But if unit economics don't work and customer acquisition is unsustainable, capital just delays death. Best approach: raise capital because you need runway, not because you want validation. Prioritize sustainable unit economics even before fundraising.

> [!tip]
> Raise capital for runway to reach profitability, not for validation. Unit economics must improve with scale, not depend on funding forever.

### Theme 2: Product-Market Fit is VCs' Primary Thesis
VCs invest in: strong founders, product-market fit signals, large TAM, defensibility. Without PMF signals (customers willing to pay, high retention, strong word-of-mouth), raising is nearly impossible or extremely dilutive. Smart founders validate PMF before aggressively fundraising. PMF signals: organic growth, high NPS, low churn, customer expansion (upselling), strong unit economics improving with scale.

> [!tip]
> Validate product-market fit before major fundraising. PMF signals: organic growth, high NPS, <5% churn, 3:1 LTV/CAC ratio, strong word-of-mouth.

### Theme 3: Founders with Domain Expertise and Founder-Market Fit
VCs prefer founders who: (1) understand the problem deeply, (2) have domain experience, (3) have built teams before, (4) maintain long-term perspective. "Founder-market fit" is critical—founders solving problems they know. If you're fundraising in unfamiliar territory, build credibility first through: advisors, partnerships, pilot customers, early revenue.

> [!tip]
> Founder expertise + domain knowledge + previous building experience = "founder-market fit." VCs weight this heavily. Build credibility before major ask.

### Theme 4: Valuation is Secondary; Terms Matter
Founders often obsess over valuation; sophisticated founders optimize for: dilution, investor quality, future fundraising impact, governance terms. High valuation at poor terms (bad board composition, unfavorable liquidation preferences, dilution) damages long-term wealth creation. Ask experienced founders for term sheet explanations. Terms matter 10x more than valuation.

> [!tip]
> Don't optimize for highest valuation. Optimize for: non-dilutive terms, quality investors, future fundraising terms, governance quality. Terms matter more than valuation.

### Theme 5: VC Incentives Align at Exit, Not During Building
VCs profit on exit; they want 10x+ returns. This aligns with growth, but not always with sustainable building. Best VCs: encourage profitability alongside growth, maintain founder focus on customers, avoid vanity metrics. Misaligned VCs: push growth at expense of unit economics, demand growth-at-any-cost, replace founders aggressively. Choose investors whose values align with yours.

> [!tip]
> VCs profit on exit. Choose investors who also value sustainable building, unit economics, and customer satisfaction alongside growth.

### Theme 6: Metrics Founders Should Track for Fundraising
VCs evaluate: MRR/ARR (monthly/annual recurring revenue), growth rate, CAC, LTV, payback period, churn, NPS. Founders should track these obsessively. Dashboard: 10-15 metrics updated weekly. Know these numbers cold before every investor meeting. Metrics tell story of business health better than narrative.

> [!tip]
> Track 15-20 key metrics weekly. Know these numbers cold. Metrics tell business health story better than pitch deck narrative.

### Theme 7: Alternatives to Venture Capital
Not every business needs or should take VC. Alternatives: bootstrap with early revenue, angel investors (smaller checks, usually lower dilution), revenue-based financing (if unit economics proven), corporate partnerships, strategic investors. If your business doesn't need $10M+ to win market, venture might not be optimal path.

> [!tip]
> Consider alternatives: bootstrapping, angels, revenue-based financing. VC is optimal only if you need $10M+ and have large TAM requiring speed to win.

## Consolidated ✅ Practical Action Steps

1. **Validate Product-Market Fit Before Major Fundraising**: Get to 10+ paying customers, measure NPS (target >50), track retention (monthly <5% churn), ensure unit economics work (LTV/CAC >3:1). These signals matter more than narrative.

2. **Build Founder Credibility**: Domain expertise, advisor board, pilot customers, early revenue. Demonstrate founder-market fit before fundraising.

3. **Understand Terms Sheet Fully**: Get experienced founder to explain terms. Key: dilution impact, liquidation preferences, board seats, governance rights. Terms matter more than valuation.

4. **Track 15-20 Key Metrics Weekly**: MRR, growth rate, CAC, LTV, payback, churn, NPS, burn rate. Know these cold. Update dashboard weekly. Metrics tell story better than pitch.

5. **Create Compelling Data Room**: Financial projections, cap table, customer data, retention analysis, unit economics. Well-organized data room signals professionalism and transparency.

6. **Choose Investors Aligned with Your Values**: Investor quality matters 10x more than capital. Choose investors who value sustainable building alongside growth, provide mentorship, respect founder autonomy.

7. **Negotiate for Future Fundraising Success**: Include: no anti-dilution on future rounds, reasonable dilution, founder-friendly governance. Future rounds are easier with clean cap table and strong investor support.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

- **Capital Enables; Unit Economics Determine Success** — Raising money gives runway; sustainable unit economics determine survival.

- **PMF Signals Beat Pitch Quality** — VCs invest in PMF evidence (growth, retention, word-of-mouth), not pitch storytelling.

- **Terms Matter More Than Valuation** — High valuation at poor terms damages long-term wealth. Optimize for founder-friendly terms.

- **Metrics Tell Story Better Than Narrative** — Know your 15-20 key metrics cold. Metrics reveal business health better than any pitch.

- **Investor Quality is Competitive Advantage** — Right investors provide mentorship, connections, credibility. Choose based on founder values alignment, not valuation alone.

# Deep Kalra: Building Profitable Tech Businesses in India

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Deep Kalra built MakeMyTrip by focusing on unit economics and profitability from day one, avoiding the VC trap of growth at all costs. Success came from understanding India's specific travel market needs (fragmented suppliers), maintaining 30%+ EBITDA margins during scaling, and strategic capital discipline. Profitability is not the opposite of growth—it's the prerequisite.

Deep Kalra is a leading entrepreneur who built MakeMyTrip into India's largest online travel platform. His expertise spans building scalable, profitable businesses, navigating market challenges, and creating sustainable growth strategies in emerging markets.

## Key Insights

### Profitability First Philosophy
- Built sustainable business focusing on unit economics from day one
- Avoided VC trap of growth at all costs
- Maintained 30%+ EBITDA margins even during scaling
- Strategic acquisition and consolidation vs pure organic growth

### Market Understanding
- India travel market had specific needs (fragmented suppliers)
- Discovery and aggregation were the core value propositions
- B2C customer acquisition expensive, B2B margins better
- Cross-selling opportunities across flights, hotels, holidays

### Business Model Strategy
- Commission-based model with suppliers (hotels, airlines)
- Dynamic pricing and yield management
- Technology as competitive moat
- Scale of operations improves supplier relationships

### Capital Efficiency
- Raised capital strategically, not opportunistically
- Maintained control through bootstrapping initially
- Exit strategy: successful IPO and acquisitions
- Capital allocation discipline across product lines

### Unit Economics as Foundation
- Unit economics don't lie—bad unit economics can't be fixed by scale alone
- Sustainable business requires positive unit economics from inception
- Every unit of growth must be profitable to scale sustainably
- Use unit economics to determine when to scale vs when to optimize

## ✅ Practical Action Steps

> [!tip]
> Concrete actions you can take TODAY.

1. **Calculate Unit Economics Today**: Determine cost to acquire a customer and their lifetime value. If negative, you need to fix the model before scaling.
2. **Test Market-Specific Solutions**: Don't copy Silicon Valley models directly. Test what works specifically in your market before scaling.
3. **Build Strategic Partnerships Early**: In travel tech, supplier relationships are moat. Map key suppliers and build relationships before competitors do.
4. **Raise Capital Strategically**: Raise when you need it, not when it's available. Control is worth more than extra runway.
5. **Focus on Acquisition Economics**: Every dollar spent acquiring a customer should return more than that dollar over customer lifetime. Optimize this ruthlessly.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

> [!note]
> Core lessons from the episode.

- **Profitability and growth are aligned, not opposed**: Growth that doesn't lead to profitability is just burning money. Build profitable unit economics first.
- **Market-specific solutions beat global templates**: Understand what India's travel market needs specifically (fragmented suppliers, affordability, discovery) and build for that.
- **Unit economics are the foundation**: Before scaling, ensure you're profitable on every unit. Scale amplifies good unit economics but destroys bad ones.
- **Technology is the competitive moat**: In travel tech, technology that improves pricing, discovery, and supplier relationships compounds into defensibility.
- **Capital discipline enables longevity**: Raising capital opportunistically rather than strategically gives you runway but can distract from profitability focus.
- **B2B and B2C economics differ**: Customer acquisition is expensive in B2C; margins and lock-in are better in B2B. Optimize the model for where you can win.
- **Scale improves supplier relationships**: Operating at scale gives you leverage with suppliers (hotels, airlines), enabling better commissions and terms than small competitors.

# College Dropout Built 100+ Cr Business - Pesto Tech Founder

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Ayush Jaiswal dropped out of college to build Pesto Tech, a coding bootcamp that transformed into a 100+ crore business. His journey highlights the importance of founder growth, learning from mistakes, managing team dynamics as companies scale, and the critical transition from hustle-driven startups to professionally run organizations.

Ayush Jaiswal is the founder of Pesto Tech, an online coding bootcamp and education platform. Starting as a college dropout with an idea to teach coding, Jaiswal scaled Pesto into a significant education company serving thousands of students across India. His experience highlights the challenges of growing both as a founder and as an organization.

## Key Insights

### Insight 1: Founder Growth and Evolution
As a founder, you will grow. Initially, hustle, doing everything yourself, and personal relationships drive the business. However, at scale (100+ employees, 100+ Cr revenue), different skills are required. The ability to recognize when you need to evolve and seek help from more experienced people is critical. Founder skills that worked at 10 people don't work at 100 people.

### Insight 2: The Hustle Trap
In early stages, hustle is necessary and valuable—putting in long hours, doing unglamorous work, solving problems on the fly. However, this same mindset can become limiting at scale. Recognizing when to move from "do everything" to "build a team that can execute" is a key inflection point. Hustle enables early progress; systems enable scale.

### Insight 3: Managing Scaling Challenges
Scaling from 10 to 100+ employees creates organizational challenges. Processes, communication structures, hiring, and culture must evolve. Many founders struggle with this transition because it requires different skill sets than early-stage building. You go from being the smartest person in the room to being the connector between people smarter than you.

### Insight 4: The Importance of Experienced Team Members
At scale, bringing in experienced operators—people who've built companies before—becomes critical. These people can take problems you've struggled with and solve them quickly. Hiring for scale requires looking beyond bright, junior people to proven operators. A mediocre person who's done it before beats a brilliant person doing it for the first time.

### Insight 5: Education Market Challenges
EdTech in India faces specific challenges: affordability (students have limited willingness to pay), placement outcomes (students judge value by job outcomes), and competition from free and cheap alternatives. Understanding these market dynamics is essential. You can't charge Silicon Valley prices in India; you must build for India's market.

### Insight 6: Building Sustainable Education Outcomes
Education is measured by outcomes—do students get jobs? Do they improve? Building an education product that consistently delivers outcomes is more complex than many founders assume. It requires curriculum design, instructor quality, and ongoing iteration. Outcomes are your competitive moat.

### Insight 7: Founder Insecurity as Motivation
A good sense of insecurity makes you stay hungry. This insecurity, channeled productively, can drive founders to continuously improve, seek feedback, and avoid complacency. However, it can also become limiting if it prevents delegation and team building. The challenge is leveraging insecurity without letting it paralyze you.

### Insight 8: The Transition from Startup to Company
There's a specific inflection point where a startup transitions to a company. This involves moving from founder-driven decisions to organizational processes, from personal relationships to professional structures. Not all founders can make this transition; recognizing if you can is important. Some founders are great at starting; others are great at scaling.

## Frameworks & Mental Models

> [!important]
> **Founder Growth Roadmap**: Early hustle → mid-stage execution → scale-stage delegation and professional management. Identify which stage you're in and what skills you need to develop.

- **Startup to Company Transition**: Moving from founder-driven culture to process-driven, professional organization. Key milestones: first manager hire, first system implementation, first organizational chart.
- **Experienced Hire Model**: Bringing in operators who've done similar things before to accelerate organizational capability. They bring patterns, not just execution.
- **Education Outcome Framework**: Defining success in education by measurable outcomes (placements, salary increases, skill acquisition). Engagement is not the same as learning.
- **Market-Specific Challenges**: Understanding dynamics specific to India's education market (affordability, competition, placement obsession). What works globally doesn't work locally.
- **Insecurity-Driven Growth**: Channeling founder insecurity into motivation for continuous improvement rather than letting it create dysfunction.

## ✅ Practical Action Steps

> [!tip]
> Concrete actions you can take TODAY based on this episode.

1. **Assess your founder skills**: Rate yourself (1-10) on: decision-making speed, team building, delegation, strategic thinking, operational excellence. Identify your weakest 2 and commit to improving them this quarter.

2. **Hire your first experienced operator**: If you're at 50+ employees and don't have a seasoned COO or operations lead, make this your next hire. Look for someone who's scaled a company to 10x your size.

3. **Document your first process**: Identify one workflow that's currently in your head (hiring, customer onboarding, product launch). Document it step-by-step. This is the start of scaling.

4. **Define your outcome metric**: For your product (if education-focused), define what success looks like for customers. Is it job placement? Salary increase? Skill level? Measure it quarterly.

5. **Schedule founder development**: Allocate 2 hours per week for your own learning—reading, mentoring, or coaching. The best investment you can make is in yourself.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

> [!note]
> Core lessons from this episode.

- **Recognize Inflection Points**: Know when to transition from hustle mode to building a professional organization; most founders miss this critical moment.
- **Invest in Founder Development**: Read books, get mentors, and deliberately work on your skills; founder improvement compounds.
- **Hire Experienced People**: Bring in people who've succeeded at the stage you're entering; they've solved problems you're about to face.
- **Create Organizational Processes**: As you scale, document processes for hiring, decision-making, and communication; this scales your leverage.
- **Measure the Right Outcomes**: Don't just optimize for growth metrics; measure what actually matters (customer results, product outcomes, unit economics).
- **Build a Professional Board**: Even if bootstrapped, develop advisory board of experienced founders who can give candid feedback.
- **Communicate Vision Consistently**: As the company grows, repeatedly communicate strategic vision so everyone understands what they're building toward.
- **Balance Confidence and Humility**: Believe in your vision but remain humble enough to learn from mistakes and feedback.

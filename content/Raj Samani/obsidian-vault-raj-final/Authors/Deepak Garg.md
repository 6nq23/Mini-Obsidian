Deepak Garg is the co-founder of Rivigo, a unicorn in the logistics and trucking sector. He spent 8 years solving real supply chain problems through relay trucking, where drivers work regional routes and handoff trucks to maintain continuous motion. Before founding Rivigo, he was a consultant at McKinsey.

## Insights by Topic

### [[Manufacturing & Export Opportunity]]
India cannot become wealthy by selling to itself—it requires manufacturing exports. China became expensive (labor costs 7-8x higher), creating a window for Indian manufacturing. Government PLI schemes support manufacturing. Unexplored opportunities exist in furniture, home decor, machine parts, automotive supply chains, appliances, toys. Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities have untapped supply chains; competition is lower in niche categories. Morbi in Gujarat is becoming a global tile manufacturing hub.

### [[The Role of Infrastructure Over Consumer Internet]]
India needs investment in data centers, cloud, telecom, ports, internet, electricity. Education and healthcare are critical for productivity gains. Manufacturing exports and B2B exports (SaaS, IT products) drive growth, not flashy consumer apps. Manufacturing doesn't get VC funding easily, but has real value creation potential. Infrastructure matters more than consumer internet saturation.

### [[Relay Trucking as Problem-Solution Fit]]
Initial product-market fit with e-commerce was strong. Real problem wasn't connecting trucks to shippers; it was driver lifestyle and utilization. Solution: relay model where drivers change every 200km, allowing trucks to run 24/7. Truck drivers prefer relay because they're home regularly vs. months-long hauls. Current shortage: 500 drivers needed per 1000 trucks; supply is drying up.

### [[Market Timing & Economic Stages]]
Study economies at your country's current stage (not the US today) to predict what works next. US in 1960s-70s saw brands emerge that could work similarly in India today. India entering wealth-creation era; not just wealth management. Next phase: leveraging productivity and manufacturing to create real national wealth.

### [[Value Chain Analysis in Tier 2/3 Cities]]
Identify unglamorous but essential problems in tier 2/3 supply chains. Build solutions there. Less VC competition in tier 2/3 means more runway to build successfully. Problem: convincing banks and VCs to fund unglamorous manufacturing businesses. Dollar revenue from exports compounds faster than rupee revenue from domestic sales.

## ✅ Practical Action Steps
> [!tip]
> 5 specific actions to identify and capture export-driven opportunities.
1. **Audit Tier 2/3 Opportunities**: Look at your city/region for supply chain gaps. What unglamorous problem exists that larger companies ignore because the market is "too small"?
2. **Study Economic Analogs**: Research China's growth in 2000s or US in 1960s-70s. What businesses succeeded then that could work similarly in India today?
3. **Talk to Ground-Level Users**: If in logistics/manufacturing, spend time with truckers, factory workers, or suppliers to understand real pain points before building.
4. **Map Your Right to Export**: Identify what you can manufacture/service for global markets. What's your competitive advantage in export markets?
5. **Build Infrastructure, Not Just Apps**: Focus on real utility (driver lifestyle, truck utilization, supply chain efficiency) rather than building another consumer app.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways
> [!note]
> 7 core lessons from Deepak Garg's journey in logistics infrastructure.
- **India's growth phase requires exports**: Domestic consumption saturation is real; wealth comes from exporting globally
- **Tier 2/3 cities are underexplored**: VCs flock to consumer internet; unglamorous manufacturing in tier 2/3 is where real value gets built
- **Study stage-appropriate comparisons**: Don't copy US/global best practices; study how similar economies evolved at India's current stage
- **Relay model validates problem understanding**: Key wasn't connecting trucks/shippers; it was understanding driver lifestyle preferences—deep consumer insight leads to product-market fit
- **Economic stage matters more than business model**: At $2K per capita, circular consumption doesn't create wealth; exports and infrastructure do
- **Patience is competitive advantage**: Manufacturing takes 3-5 years to gain traction; VCs lacking patience create opportunity for disciplined builders
- **Unglamorous businesses create real wealth**: Every country that got rich did it through manufacturing. Don't dismiss "boring" sectors if they solve real problems

Siddharth Jain (Karan Singh) is CEO of Sunburn Festival, India's most established and awarded EDM festival. Sunburn was ranked 10th best festival in the world by DJ Mag—the first Asian festival to achieve this ranking. Started in college party organizing, Sunburn now hosts 40,000+ people per day with multiple international artists, and is expanding into lifestyle brand merchandise, F&B franchises, and international markets.

## Insights by Topic

### [[The Real Costs Behind Music Festivals]]
40-50% Artist Costs: Headliners cost ₹100,000-₹400,000+ each; top tier cost ₹40 lakhs+. 20-30% Production Costs: Stages, LED screens, sound systems, technical inventory, CO2 jets, pyrotechnics, setup from scratch. Remaining 20-30%: Licensing, F&B, logistics, security, medical, contingencies. Revenue: 40,000 people/day × ₹2,000-2,500/ticket = Appears huge but razor-thin margins. Forex Risk: Currency fluctuations can wipe out entire margins if international artists aren't locked in early.

### [[Artist Booking Economics]]
Booking timeline: Must start 11-12 months ahead; artists make schedules in advance. All-inclusive fees: Include flights, hotels, crew (6-10 people per artist: production, lighting, security, marketing, photo/video). Revenue share models: Guarantee model (fixed fee against 80% of ticket sales); split excess above threshold. Artist Riders: Beyond technical specs, include bizarre requests (Avicii wanted Lego sets, PlayStation, football).

### [[Why Sunburn Succeeded While Others Failed]]
15-year brand building: No shortcuts; experiential brand recognition that competitors can't replicate overnight. Data-driven decisions: Analyze repeat customer rates, macro demographics (youth population, colleges), ticket velocity by city. Attention to execution detail: While competitors focus on booking, Sunburn focuses on logistics, parking, crowd management, experience. Professional production even while losing money: Never cut corners on stage, sound, artist treatment—builds reputation.

### [[Production Challenges & Scale Management]]
Scale becomes challenge: Once you exceed 7,000-10,000 people, crowd management becomes exponentially harder. On-ground production timeline: Ideation year-round; actual production 1 month before event; 1,500-2,000 ground staff required. Licensing complexity: 6-8+ licenses needed (police, traffic, excise, fire safety, coastal regulations in Goa, tourism). Bureaucracy is the real challenge: Less politics, more bureaucratic red tape; lack of standardization.

### [[Key Security & Safety Challenges]]
Organized phone theft: Gangs buy tickets and steal smartphones; caught through exit screening. Crowd management: At 40,000 people, fights, alcohol abuse, people losing friends are common. Mini hospital infrastructure: On-site medical facility essential; can't rely on hospitals 30+ km away.

## ✅ Practical Action Steps
> [!tip]
> 5 actions to apply event economics and brand-building to your venture.
1. **Model your event economics**: Create a P&L for your event. Artist costs should be 35-45% of ticket revenue. Production should be 20-30%. Identify your margin (typically 5-15%). If higher, you're cutting corners.
2. **Develop a vendor spreadsheet**: List all vendors (artists, production, logistics, security, medical) with costs. Identify which ones have most upside/downside risk. Negotiate early and lock in prices.
3. **Create a licensing checklist**: Identify all licenses you need (permits, insurance, regulatory approvals). Assign owners and create a Gantt chart. Don't leave licensing to the last month.
4. **Design a customer feedback loop**: After your event, survey 500 attendees on experience (not just artist lineup). Measure NPS (Net Promoter Score). Track repeat attendance. This is your brand equity.
5. **Build a contingency plan**: For your biggest risk (artist cancellation, weather, crowd overflow), create a specific action plan. Test it with your team before D-Day.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways
> [!note]
> Core lessons from this episode.
- **Thin Margins Require Cost Discipline**: 40-50% artist costs means precision in all other areas. Vendor management and operational efficiency are competitive advantages
- **Brand > Lineup**: Initially, famous artists attract attendees. Long-term success requires operational excellence and memorable experiences
- **Play for the Long Game**: Invest years building brand reputation. There are no shortcuts to trust
- **Operational Detail is Advantage**: While competitors focus on booking, dominate through logistics, crowd management, safety
- **Manage Immovable Deadlines**: Build processes that guarantee execution regardless of obstacles. Events happen on their date—no extensions possible
- **Data-Driven Expansion**: Use macro indicators (youth population, college density) + historical benchmarks to expand
- **Diversify Beyond Events**: Merge into lifestyle brand (merchandise, F&B franchises) to reduce event-only risk

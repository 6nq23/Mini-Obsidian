# Karan Singh (Sunburn CEO)

> [!abstract]
> **Who:** Karan Singh is the CEO and visionary behind Sunburn Festival, India's most awarded and globally recognized EDM (Electronic Dance Music) festival. Starting from college party organizing, he scaled Sunburn to 40,000+ daily attendees and ranked 10th best festival globally by DJ Mag—the first Asian festival to achieve this status. Reveals the hidden economics of live events: artist costs (40-50%), production (20-30%), and operational challenges.

## Key Insights from Karan Singh

### On Event Economics and Thin Margins
The music festival business operates on surprisingly thin margins despite massive revenue. Artist costs consume 40-50% of ticket revenue; top-tier international artists (Swedish House Mafia, The Chainsmokers) cost ₹40 lakhs+. Production costs (stages, LED screens, sound systems, CO2 jets, pyrotechnics) take 20-30%. Remaining 20-30% covers licensing, F&B, logistics, security, medical, contingencies. Forex fluctuations can wipe out entire margins if international artists aren't locked in early.

> [!tip]
> In razor-thin margin businesses, operational excellence and vendor management are competitive advantages. Detail matters more than scale.

### On Brand Building and Long-Term Thinking
Sunburn succeeded where competitors failed because Karan played for 15+ year brand-building game. No shortcuts. Experiential brand recognition can't be replicated overnight. While competitors focused on booking lineups, Sunburn obsessed over logistics, crowd management, parking, customer experience. Professional production quality maintained even while losing money—this builds reputation long-term.

> [!tip]
> Brand building takes years of consistent investment. Don't cut corners on quality. Quality compounds into brand equity.

### On Data-Driven Decision Making
Sunburn analyzed repeat customer rates, macro demographics (youth population, colleges per city), ticket velocity by geography. This data-driven approach enabled better city selection for expansion and customer targeting. Analyzed artist performance (which artists drive tickets, which are expensive but don't drive volume).

> [!tip]
> Track data on customer behavior, unit economics per artist, geographic performance. Data guides expansion decisions.

### On Production Challenges and Scale
Once Sunburn exceeded 7,000-10,000 people, crowd management became exponentially harder. On-ground production requires: year-round ideation, 1-month intense on-ground execution, 1,500-2,000 ground staff. Licensing complexity: 6-8+ licenses needed (police, traffic, excise, fire safety, coastal regulations in Goa, tourism). Bureaucracy is the real challenge—not politics, but red tape and lack of standardization.

> [!tip]
> Understand regulatory requirements early. Build licenses and relationships year-round. Don't leave compliance to last month.

### On Artist Booking Economics
Must start artist booking 11-12 months ahead; artists plan schedules far in advance. Artist fees are all-inclusive: flights, hotels, crew (6-10 people: production, lighting, security, marketing, photo/video). Revenue share models: guarantee model (fixed fee against 80% ticket sales) or split excess above threshold. Artist riders include bizarre requests (Aviciji wanted Lego sets, PlayStation, football).

> [!tip]
> In business with vendor dependencies, long booking windows force early financial commitments. Lock-in early to manage forex and artist costs.

### On Word-of-Mouth Marketing
"You can only rely on word-of-mouth marketing. If my five friends say the event was fantastic, I want to go." Paid marketing helps, but organic word-of-mouth and repeat attendance build lasting business. Customer experience—not just artist lineup—drives word-of-mouth.

> [!tip]
> Customer experience drives word-of-mouth more than marketing spend. Invest in experience quality; marketing compounds.

### On Live Event Industry Realities
"In the live event industry, there are no shortcuts. You have to live through the learnings, make the same mistakes, face every eventuality." Attention to detail is everything—you can't ask for an extension when the event happens on October 15th. Immovable deadline forces execution excellence.

> [!tip]
> In execution-dependent businesses, attention to detail separates winners from losers. Practice and preparation compound.

## ✅ Practical Action Steps from Karan Singh
> [!tip]

1. **Model Your Business Economics**: Create detailed P&L. Know your unit economics. Artist costs should be 35-45% of revenue; production 20-30%. Identify your margin. If higher, you're cutting corners.

2. **Develop Vendor Spreadsheet**: List vendors with costs. Identify upside/downside risk. Negotiate early. Lock in prices before forex fluctuation or inflation.

3. **Create Licensing Checklist**: Identify all licenses needed. Assign owners. Create Gantt chart. Don't leave licensing to last month—bureaucracy is slow.

4. **Design Customer Feedback Loop**: After event, survey 500 attendees on experience (not just lineup). Measure NPS. Track repeat attendance. This is brand equity.

5. **Build Contingency Plans**: For biggest risk (artist cancellation, weather, crowd overflow), create action plan. Test with team before D-Day.

6. **Invest in On-Ground Execution**: Quality production and customer experience drive word-of-mouth more than artist lineup. Don't cheap out on execution.

7. **Long-Term Vendor Relationships**: Build relationships with key vendors year-round. These relationships save you when things go wrong.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways
> [!note]

- **Thin Margins Require Cost Discipline**: Know exact unit economics. Vendor management and operational efficiency are competitive advantages.
- **Brand Takes 15+ Years**: No shortcuts to trusted brand. Play long-term game. Invest in quality even while losing money early.
- **Operational Excellence Wins**: While competitors focus on flashy artists, winners focus on crowd management, logistics, customer experience.
- **Data Drives Decisions**: Track customer behavior, artist performance, geographic performance. Data guides expansion.
- **Regulatory Navigation Critical**: Build licenses and relationships early. Red tape is real challenge—plan accordingly.
- **Word-of-Mouth Compounds**: Customer experience drives organic growth. Paid marketing accelerates but can't replace quality experience.
- **Execution Beats Strategy**: With immovable deadlines, execution discipline and attention to detail separate winners from losers.

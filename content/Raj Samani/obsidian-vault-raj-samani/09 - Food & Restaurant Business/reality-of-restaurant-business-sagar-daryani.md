# The Reality of Restaurant Business in India - Licenses, Margins & Aggregators

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Operating a restaurant in India requires 32+ licenses versus only 13 for gun ownership, and about 60% of revenue goes to taxes and licensing fees. Success requires strategic location selection, simple menus that build trust through location, and understanding that consumer education costs money.

## Guest Overview
Sagar Daryani is an experienced restaurant entrepreneur who has launched multiple dining concepts in India. He provides frank insights into the regulatory burdens, margins, and competitive dynamics of the restaurant business, including the impact of aggregators like Zomato and Swiggy on restaurant profitability.

## Key Insights

### The Regulatory Maze: 32 Licenses for a Restaurant
The regulatory burden in India is severe. Opening a restaurant requires 32 different licenses across food safety, health, labor, building permits, trade registration, and more. This regulatory complexity is a significant barrier to entry and a massive drain on time and resources for restaurant operators.

### Tax and License Fee Burden: 60% of Revenue
Roughly 60% of restaurant revenue goes toward taxes and licensing fees. This leaves restaurants with thin operating margins, making scale and efficiency critical to profitability. This is a fundamental constraint that shapes restaurant unit economics.

### Location Selection is Everything
Success begins with choosing the right location. A perfect location can make a mediocre restaurant successful, while a bad location will kill an excellent one. Location determines foot traffic, customer demographics, and whether customers will develop the habit of visiting.

### Simple Menu, Strong Brand Communication
Instead of complex menus that confuse customers, successful restaurants focus on simple, high-quality offerings. When a customer sees "Wao Momos" sold at ₹20-50, they immediately understand the concept. Pricing clarity and menu simplicity build consumer confidence.

### Building Consumer Habits Costs Money
Consumers don't walk into unknown restaurants easily. Building the habit requires consistent customer experience, location visibility, and often local word-of-mouth. This education phase is expensive and requires patience.

### Location-Based Product Positioning
The same restaurant concept won't work in all locations. A ₹50 momo stand will succeed on a busy street but fail in a premium mall. Understanding location dynamics and customer willingness-to-pay is critical.

### Aggregator Dependency Risk
Zomato and Swiggy have become essential distribution channels, but they also create dependency risks. Restaurants become price-takers on these platforms, and aggregator commissions (up to 30%+) further compress margins.

### Franchise vs. Company-Operated Models
Franchising allows rapid expansion without capital requirements, but quality control becomes challenging. Company-operated stores maintain consistency but require more capital and management resources.

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Location First, Concept Second**: Pick an excellent location with traffic and demographics aligned to your concept, then build a simple menu that serves that location perfectly.

- **Regulatory Cost Built-in**: Budget 60% of revenue for taxes, licenses, and compliance. This isn't negotiable in India. Build unit economics assuming this constraint.

- **Habit Formation Timeline**: Budget 6-12 months for customers to develop the habit of visiting your location. During this phase, expect operational losses.

- **Aggregator as Channel, Not Master**: Use Zomato and Swiggy as channels but don't become dependent. Maintain owned channels (location traffic, own delivery, loyalty program) to reduce aggregator dependency.

- **Simple > Complex**: A simple concept executed excellently beats a complex menu executed poorly. Simplicity also improves training consistency across franchises.

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs & Builders

- **Location is Non-Negotiable**: Spend time and money finding the right location. It's the foundation of restaurant success.

- **Regulatory Preparedness**: Budget for all 32+ licenses upfront. Don't be surprised by licensing costs. Consider hiring a regulatory consultant.

- **Menu Strategy**: Start with a focused menu (3-5 core items) and expand only after validating each item. Complex menus increase waste and training complexity.

- **Customer Acquisition Patience**: Don't expect profitability in year one. Customer habit formation takes time, especially for new concepts in new locations.

- **Build Owned Channels**: While using aggregators, invest in owned customer relationships through location visibility, loyalty programs, and owned delivery.

- **Understand Local Preferences**: What works in Mumbai might not work in Delhi. Spend time understanding local food preferences, price expectations, and competitive dynamics.

- **Franchising Requires Systems**: If planning to franchise, build repeatable systems and quality control mechanisms first. Don't franchise to fund growth; franchise to accelerate a proven concept.

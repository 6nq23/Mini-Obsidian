# College Dropout Built 100+ Cr Business - Pesto Tech Founder

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Ayush Jaiswal dropped out of college to build Pesto Tech, a coding bootcamp that transformed into a 100+ crore business. His journey highlights the importance of founder growth, learning from mistakes, managing team dynamics as companies scale, and the critical transition from hustle-driven startups to professionally run organizations.

## Guest Overview

Ayush Jaiswal is the founder of Pesto Tech, an online coding bootcamp and education platform. Starting as a college dropout with an idea to teach coding, Jaiswal scaled Pesto into a significant education company serving thousands of students across India. His experience highlights the challenges of growing both as a founder and as an organization.

## Key Insights

### Founder Growth and Evolution
As a founder, you will grow. Initially, hustle, doing everything yourself, and personal relationships drive the business. However, at scale (100+ employees, 100+ Cr revenue), different skills are required. The ability to recognize when you need to evolve and seek help from more experienced people is critical.

### The Hustle Trap
In early stages, hustle is necessary and valuable—putting in long hours, doing unglamorous work, solving problems on the fly. However, this same mindset can become limiting at scale. Recognizing when to move from "do everything" to "build a team that can execute" is a key inflection point.

### Managing Scaling Challenges
Scaling from 10 to 100+ employees creates organizational challenges. Processes, communication structures, hiring, and culture must evolve. Many founders struggle with this transition because it requires different skill sets than early-stage building.

### The Importance of Experienced Team Members
At scale, bringing in experienced operators—people who've built companies before—becomes critical. These people can take problems you've struggled with and solve them quickly. Hiring for scale requires looking beyond bright, junior people to proven operators.

### Education Market Challenges
EdTech in India faces specific challenges: affordability (students have limited willingness to pay), placement outcomes (students judge value by job outcomes), and competition from free and cheap alternatives. Understanding these market dynamics is essential.

### Building Sustainable Education Outcomes
Education is measured by outcomes—do students get jobs? Do they improve? Building an education product that consistently delivers outcomes is more complex than many founders assume. It requires curriculum design, instructor quality, and ongoing iteration.

### Founder Insecurity as Motivation
Pitti mentioned that "a good sense of insecurity makes you stay hungry." This insecurity, channeled productively, can drive founders to continuously improve, seek feedback, and avoid complacency. However, it can also become limiting if it prevents delegation and team building.

### The Transition from Startup to Company
There's a specific inflection point where a startup transitions to a company. This involves moving from founder-driven decisions to organizational processes, from personal relationships to professional structures. Not all founders can make this transition; recognizing if you can is important.

### Learning from Mistakes at Scale
Early mistakes cost less (money, opportunity, reputation). However, at scale, mistakes compound. Learning to make better decisions faster, taking advice from experienced people, and implementing rigorous decision-making processes becomes critical.

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Founder Growth Roadmap**: Early hustle → mid-stage execution → scale-stage delegation and professional management
- **Startup to Company Transition**: Moving from founder-driven culture to process-driven, professional organization
- **Experienced Hire Model**: Bringing in operators who've done similar things before to accelerate organizational capability
- **Education Outcome Framework**: Defining success in education by measurable outcomes (placements, salary increases, skill acquisition)
- **Market-Specific Challenges**: Understanding dynamics specific to India's education market (affordability, competition, placement obsession)
- **Insecurity-Driven Growth**: Channeling founder insecurity into motivation for continuous improvement rather than letting it create dysfunction

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs / Builders

- **Recognize inflection points** - Know when to transition from hustle mode to building a professional organization; most founders miss this
- **Invest in founder development** - Read books, get mentors, and deliberately work on your skills; founder improvement compounds
- **Hire experienced people** - Bring in people who've succeeded at the stage you're entering; they've solved problems you're about to face
- **Create organizational processes** - As you scale, document processes for hiring, decision-making, and communication; this scales your leverage
- **Measure the right outcomes** - Don't just optimize for growth metrics; measure what actually matters (customer retention, product outcomes, unit economics)
- **Build a professional board** - Even if you're bootstrapped, develop advisory board of experienced founders who can give you candid feedback
- **Communicate vision consistently** - As the company grows, repeatedly communicate strategic vision so everyone understands what they're building toward
- **Create space for learning** - Budget time and money for your own learning; the cost of unlearned lessons is higher at scale
- **Balance confidence and humility** - Believe in your vision but remain humble enough to learn from mistakes and feedback
- **Plan for team scaling** - Anticipate how organizational structure needs to evolve; don't wait for dysfunction to force changes

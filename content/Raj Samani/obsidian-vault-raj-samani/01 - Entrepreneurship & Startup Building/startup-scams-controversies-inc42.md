# Startup Scams and Controversies - Inside Look at Startup Ecosystem

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Inc42's Rajan Anandan provides insider perspective on startup controversies, scams, and failures in the Indian ecosystem, particularly focusing on the Byju's vs. Aakash competition, media business dynamics, and threats facing startups that prioritize growth over governance.

## Guest Overview

Rajan Anandan is a co-founder of Inc42, India's leading startup news and intelligence platform. With years of covering the Indian startup ecosystem, Anandan has observed both the triumphs and failures of startups firsthand. He provides candid analysis of what goes wrong, how governance failures lead to collapse, and what the ecosystem should focus on.

## Key Insights

### The Byju's Collapse Story
Byju's serves as a cautionary tale in India's startup ecosystem. The company grew rapidly through aggressive marketing and capital deployment, but underlying issues—aggressive customer acquisition practices, high churn rates, operational challenges—eventually caught up. The company prioritized growth and valuations over sustainable business building.

### Deceptive Practices Hide Bad Unit Economics
Many startups use misleading customer acquisition tactics, high refund rates, and aggressive sales practices to mask bad unit economics. Rather than fixing the underlying business model, they continue spending on marketing to acquire customers who often don't stay or use the product. This is unsustainable.

### Media Narrative vs. Reality
Startup media (including Inc42) often covers exciting growth stories and funding announcements. However, the reality of what's happening operationally—customer churn, unit economics, internal conflicts—often paints a different picture. Founders should see beyond media coverage.

### Byju's vs. Aakash Competition
The competition between Byju's (D2C edtech) and Aakash (tuition center model) revealed different business models. Byju's tried to replace tutoring through technology and scale nationally, while Aakash built a network of physical centers. The competition showed that technology alone doesn't guarantee victory if the business model is flawed.

### EdTech's Fundamental Challenges
EdTech in India faces structural challenges: low willingness to pay for most online courses, high churn rates, and the reality that human interaction (tutoring) remains valuable. Many EdTech startups haven't solved these fundamental challenges.

### Governance Failures Lead to Collapse
A common theme in startup failures is governance breakdown. Boards that don't function, VCs who overlook red flags, and founders who lose accountability—these governance issues precede financial collapse. By the time money runs out, operational dysfunction has already set in.

### The Role of Capital in Masking Issues
Massive capital inflows can mask operational issues for a while. A company with bad unit economics can keep growing by burning capital, but eventually, the capital runs out. The startup ecosystem sometimes celebrates this as "blitzscaling" when it's actually unsustainable.

### Media Business Challenges
Inc42 itself operates in the media business, which has its own challenges. The tension between covering news objectively and maintaining relationships with companies (who might advertise or sponsor) creates conflicts. A media business model that's sustainable is harder to build than it seems.

### Threats to the Ecosystem
Anandan identified several threats: regulatory crackdown on misleading practices, consumer backlash against scams, VC funding drying up, and a reckoning coming for companies with bad fundamentals. The next phase of Indian startup ecosystem will be less forgiving than past.

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Unit Economics Reality Check**: Compare what companies claim about profitability vs. actual unit economics; if they don't align, something is wrong
- **Governance as Leading Indicator**: Strong governance (functional board, clear accountability, regular audits) is a leading indicator of financial health
- **Capital vs. Sustainability**: Track how long a company can survive at current burn rate; if capital is masking unsustainable unit economics, it will eventually collapse
- **Media Narrative Skepticism**: Understand that startup media coverage emphasizes exciting stories; read between the lines for operational realities
- **Business Model Validity**: For each business model (D2C, marketplace, service), understand what sustainable unit economics look like and whether a company is trending toward them

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs / Builders

- **Don't rely on capital to fix bad fundamentals** - No amount of VC money can indefinitely subsidize bad unit economics; fix the business model first
- **Prioritize governance from early stages** - Functional boards, clear accountability, and regular audits prevent later surprises
- **Be honest about unit economics** - Don't hide bad unit economics behind growth stories; understand and fix them
- **Customer acquisition quality matters** - Focus on acquiring customers who stay and derive value, not customers you have to keep re-acquiring
- **Watch for red flags in competitors** - When competitors are spending excessively on marketing or having high churn, that's a signal of underlying problems
- **Build sustainable, not just scalable** - Scalability means growing while maintaining unit economics; pure growth at all costs is unsustainable
- **Understand your business model deeply** - Know what profitability looks like for your category and trend toward it consistently
- **Engage with critical media** - Don't just seek positive coverage; engage with critical perspectives that help identify gaps in your approach
- **Build for long-term, not just the next funding round** - Decision-making oriented toward raising more capital often misaligns with building sustainable businesses

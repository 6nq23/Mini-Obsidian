# European Billionaire in India: Marketing, Leadership, Hiring & Odoo

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Europe's high tax burden (77% in Belgium) makes India attractive for scaling. Offline marketing is underrated. Incompetent middle management destroys teams. Bad managers serve themselves; good leaders serve their teams. India has cheap resources and can become a global power if it prioritizes efficiency and quality.

## Guest Overview
Dominic Cools is the founder of Odoo, a European enterprise software company valued in the billions. He brings a unique perspective on building a European-born company, the challenges of operating in high-tax environments, and why India represents both an opportunity and a testing ground for global expansion.

## Key Insights

### The Economics of Operating in Europe vs. India
- In Belgium, 77% of gross income goes to the government as taxes
- This high tax burden makes India attractive for lower-cost operations and higher margins
- Ability to sell to US and European markets at higher prices with low Indian cost base = sustainable competitive advantage
- India has not yet understood the power of its cheap resources + global market access

### The Competency Crisis in Indian Management
- Most middle managers in India are incompetent
- A bad manager treats the team as servants of the manager
- A good team leader positions themselves as servants of the team—coaching, training, developing people
- This incompetence is a systemic problem, not individual failure

### Underrated Offline Marketing
- Digital marketing (Google Ads, KPIs, etc.) dominates Indian marketing discourse
- Offline marketing (billboards, events, in-person) is severely underrated because impact is hard to measure
- In Europe, offline marketing works exceptionally well but is underutilized
- Mixing measured digital performance with unmeasurable but high-impact offline creates better results

### Why Zoho Has No Serious Competitors
- Zoho is the biggest player in Indian software because it built efficiency and quality at scale
- No Indian competitor has matched Zoho's ability to think big and execute globally
- Zoho didn't need Silicon Valley validation to succeed

### Building Global-Quality Products in India
- If Indians understood how to become "really really efficient," India could become a global superpower
- Current challenge: balancing low costs with world-class quality
- Efficiency applies to product, operations, customer service, and marketing

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Tax Arbitrage Model**: Low-cost operations in high-tax-friendly countries + global market access = sustainable margins
- **Manager vs. Leader Spectrum**: Rank your team leadership on a scale from "serves themselves" to "serves the team"
- **Marketing Channel Mix**: Don't rely solely on measurable digital; factor in unmeasurable offline impact for brand building

## Notable Quotes

> "In Belgium, 77% of the gross goes to the pocket of the government. In India, you can sell to the US and Europe at much higher prices, which makes you comfortable very quickly with high margins."

> "So much incompetent managers—they have to be team leaders. A bad manager is a team in service of the manager. A good team leader is in service of the team, coaching and training them."

> "Offline marketing is underrated. Most people don't know it works really well in Europe because there are tons of marketers who don't understand what they do."

> "If Indians start understanding this strength we have of cheap resources and become really efficient, then we can be the global powers."

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs & Builders

- Leverage India's cost advantage to build profitable global businesses
- Invest in developing quality middle management—it's a leverage point
- Don't over-rely on performance marketing; use offline for brand building
- Think globally from day one; build for export markets, not just India
- Understand that efficiency is a competitive advantage, not just cost reduction
- Study Zoho's playbook: global ambition, execution-focused, no VC dependency

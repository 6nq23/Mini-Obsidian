# DIRTY Secrets of Music Business Explained by Sunburn CEO

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Sunburn CEO reveals why music festivals operate on thin margins despite massive revenue: artist costs consume 40-50% of budget, production costs 20-30%, and operational/licensing costs eat the rest. Success requires long-term brand building (15+ years), world-class execution even while losing money, data-driven decisions, and attention to operational detail that competitors overlook.

## Guest Overview
Siddharth Jain is CEO of Sunburn Festival, India's most established and awarded EDM festival. Sunburn was ranked 10th best festival in the world by DJ Mag—the first Asian festival to achieve this ranking. Started in college party organizing, Sunburn now hosts 40,000+ people per day with multiple international artists, and is expanding into lifestyle brand merchandise, F&B franchises, and international markets.

## Key Insights

### The Real Costs Behind Music Festivals: Why Margins Are Thin
- **40-50% Artist Costs**: Headliners cost ₹100,000-₹400,000+ each; top tier (Swedish House Mafia, The Chainsmokers) cost ₹40 lakhs+
- **20-30% Production Costs**: Stages, LED screens, sound systems, technical inventory, CO2 jets, pyrotechnics, setup from scratch
- **Remaining 20-30%**: Licensing, F&B, logistics, security, medical, contingencies
- **Revenue**: 40,000 people/day × ₹2,000-2,500/ticket = Appears huge but razor-thin margins
- **Forex Risk**: Currency fluctuations can wipe out entire margins if international artists aren't locked in early

### Artist Booking Economics
- **Booking timeline**: Must start 11-12 months ahead; artists make schedules in advance
- **All-inclusive fees**: Include flights, hotels, crew (6-10 people per artist: production, lighting, security, marketing, photo/video)
- **Most expensive**: Swedish House Mafia, The Chainsmokers (₹40+ lakhs); Wiz Khalifa cheaper than expected despite fame
- **Revenue share models**:
  - Guarantee model: Fixed fee of ₹X against 80% of ticket sales; if sales exceed guarantee, split excess
  - Example: ₹2 lakh fee, plan to sell 12,000 tickets for 15% margin; if 15,000 sell, artist gets 2/3 of extra 3,000 tickets
- **Artist Riders**: Beyond technical specs, include bizarre requests (Avicii wanted Lego sets, PlayStation, football; Justin Bieber requested premium massage services)

### Why Sunburn Succeeded While Others Failed
- **15-year brand building**: No shortcuts; experiential brand recognition that competitors can't replicate overnight
- **Data-driven decisions**: Analyze repeat customer rates, macro demographics (youth population, colleges), ticket velocity by city
- **Attention to execution detail**: While competitors focus on booking, Sunburn focuses on logistics, parking, crowd management, experience
- **Professional production even while losing money**: Never cut corners on stage, sound, artist treatment—builds reputation
- **World-class hospitality**: Give international artists gifts, excellent service so they return voluntarily

### Production Challenges & Scale Management
- **Scale becomes challenge**: Once you exceed 7,000-10,000 people, crowd management becomes exponentially harder
- **On-ground production timeline**: Ideation year-round; actual production 1 month before event; 1,500-2,000 ground staff required
- **Licensing complexity**: 6-8+ licenses needed (police, traffic, excise, fire safety, coastal regulations in Goa, tourism)
- **Last-minute issues**: Licenses often come last-minute; all-or-nothing execution date; no extensions possible
- **Bureaucracy is the real challenge**: Less politics, more bureaucratic red tape; lack of standardization compared to Western venues with pre-licensed spaces

### Artist Selection & Exclusivity Myths
- **No formal exclusivity**: International artists and agents won't give you formal exclusivity contracts
- **Relationship-based loyalty**: Artists return if you deliver world-class production, handle them professionally, expand their footprint in India
- **Strategic partnerships**: Work with artists on multi-city tours (Delhi, Bangalore, Mumbai) and PR activities during off-days
- **Market expansion strategy**: Offer artists reduced per-show fees but more shows (6-8 shows with one artist vs. 1-2)

### Key Security & Safety Challenges
- **Organized phone theft**: Gangs buy tickets and steal smartphones; caught through exit screening in recent incidents
- **Crowd management**: At 40,000 people, fights, alcohol abuse, people losing friends are common
- **Mini hospital infrastructure**: On-site medical facility essential; can't rely on hospitals 30+ km away (traffic issues)
- **Curfew constraints**: Hard 10 PM cutoff (law); can't extend regardless of circumstances; disrupts international artists' timelines
- **Drug concerns less severe in India**: EDM/young audience culture in India less prone to hard drug use vs. Western metal/rock festivals

### Ticketing & Logistics Innovation
- **Fake ticket problem**: Solved through BookMyShow protections (barcodes, e-tickets, wristbands with NFC)
- **Home delivery of wristbands**: Pre-loaded with F&B balance; reduces hassle of on-site queues
- **Entry optimization**: Wristbands with pre-loaded balance means people don't need to carry cash; faster entry

### Challenges Specific to India
- **Infrastructure from scratch**: Western venues pre-licensed for 10,000+ people; Sunburn builds everything each event (toilets, bars, entry gates, barricades)
- **Traffic and parking**: Major variable; can add 1.5+ hours to arrival
- **Weather constraints**: 6-8 PM window when people can actually come (too hot/humid before); everyone arrives compressed, everyone leaves at 10 PM (hard curfew)
- **Incomplete data**: Can't get perfect demographic data; must infer from macro indicators (other events, concert attendance)

## Frameworks & Mental Models
- **Artist Cost Equation**: Base guarantee + Forex hedge + Escalating margins at higher ticket volumes
- **Event Economics Timeline**: Year-round ideation → 1 month on-ground execution → All-or-nothing D-Day (immovable deadline)
- **Experience vs. Lineup**: Reputation initially depends on artist lineup; long-term retention depends on operational experience
- **Scaling Principle**: Attention to detail and hands-on execution matters more than scale theory; rolls up your sleeves
- **Data-Driven Expansion**: Macro indicators (youth population, college density) + historical benchmarks (other events' performance)
- **Brand Value**: Trusted artists will choose Sunburn over higher-paying competitors because of guaranteed execution quality

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs / Builders
- **Customer experience beats line-up**: Initially, famous artists attract attendees. Long-term success requires operational excellence and memorable experiences
- **Play for the long game**: Invest years building brand reputation; there are no shortcuts to trust
- **Build data discipline early**: Don't rely on gut; analyze repeat rates, demographics, competition, ticket velocity
- **Operational detail is competitive advantage**: While competitors focus on big-picture strategy, dominate through logistics, crowd management, safety
- **Work partnerships, not exclusivity**: Build win-win relationships with partners/artists; offer them value (expanded tours, better cities) instead of restrictive contracts
- **Manage immovable deadlines**: Build processes that guarantee execution regardless of obstacles; no extensions possible in event business
- **Thin margins require cost discipline**: 40-50% costs in one category (artists) means precision in all others; forex hedging, vendor management, operational efficiency matter
- **Diversify beyond events**: Merge into lifestyle brand (merchandise, F&B franchises, experiences) to reduce event-only risk

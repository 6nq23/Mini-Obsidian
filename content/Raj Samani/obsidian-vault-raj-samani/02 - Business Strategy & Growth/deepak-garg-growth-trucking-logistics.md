# Deepak Garg on Growth Strategies, Profitable Business & Scalability

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** India cannot become wealthy by selling to itself—it needs manufacturing exports and productivity improvements. Traditional logistics can be modernized through technology. The real opportunity lies in building businesses for export markets, not competing in saturated consumer internet.

## Guest Overview
Deepak Garg is the co-founder of Rivigo, a unicorn in the unsexy but essential trucking and logistics sector. He spent 8 years solving real supply chain problems through relay trucking, where drivers work regional routes and handoff trucks to maintain continuous motion. Before founding Rivigo, he was a consultant at McKinsey.

## Key Insights

### Why India Can't Get Rich Through Consumer Spending
- India has a per capita income of ~$2,000, needs to hit $10,000 before consumer spending becomes meaningful
- China crossed $10,000 in 2012, which is when Alibaba and consumer brands exploded
- The cap market in India got saturated not through genuine demand but through incentives and subsidies
- Real wealth creation requires exporting goods and services, not circular consumption within the domestic economy

### Manufacturing & Export Opportunity
- India has a window NOW because China has become expensive (labor costs 7-8x higher)
- Government PLI schemes support manufacturing across sectors
- Unexplored opportunities: furniture, home decor, machine parts, automotive supply chains, appliances, toys
- Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities have massive untapped supply chains; competition is less in niche categories
- Example: Morbi in Gujarat is becoming a global tile manufacturing hub

### The Role of Web 2 vs Web 3 Centralization
- Web 2 tech starts "for good" but inevitably consolidates power due to shareholder pressure
- Centralized platforms enable speed but create dependency (Uber, Netflix struggle in India)
- Web 3 offers decentralization but sacrifices decision-making speed
- Neither system is perfect; both have tradeoffs between innovation speed and distributed benefits

### Why Infrastructure Matters More Than Consumer Internet
- India needs to invest in: data centers, cloud, telecom, ports, internet, electricity
- Education and healthcare are critical for productivity gains
- Manufacturing exports and B2B exports (SaaS, IT products) will drive growth, not flashy consumer apps
- Manufacturing doesn't get VC funding easily, but has real value creation potential

### Finding Market Fit in Tier 2/3 Cities
- Study what worked at similar development stages: US 1960s-70s, China in 2000s
- Look at how brands entered markets; understand fundamentals of economic evolution
- Less VC competition in tier 2/3 means more runway to build successfully
- Problem: convincing banks and VCs to fund unglamorous manufacturing businesses

### Relay Trucking as a Case Study
- Initial product-market fit with e-commerce (Amazon/Flipkart) was strong
- Real problem wasn't connecting trucks to shippers; it was driver lifestyle and utilization
- Solution: relay model where drivers change every 200km, allowing trucks to run 24/7
- Truck drivers prefer relay because they're home regularly vs. months-long hauls
- Current shortage: 500 drivers needed per 1000 trucks; supply is drying up

### Market Timing and Macro Conditions
- India will unlock value when it enters the "wealth creation era"—not just wealth management
- Current phase: 1-2 crore retail accounts just added; people starting to invest
- Next phase: leveraging productivity and manufacturing to create real national wealth
- Rivigo positioned as logistics infrastructure for export-driven businesses

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Value Chain Analysis**: Identify unglamorous but essential problems in tier 2/3 supply chains; build solutions there
- **Market Development Stage Mapping**: Study economies at your country's current stage (not the US today) to predict what works next
- **Network Effects in Logistics**: Relay model creates incentives for all parties (drivers, fleet owners, shippers) to participate
- **The Export Multiplier**: Dollar revenue from exports compounds faster than rupee revenue from domestic sales

## Notable Quotes

> "We can't get rich by selling stuff to each other. That's been the story of China, Japan, South Korea. Every country that got rich did it through manufacturing and exports."

> "You're comparing US today with India today—that's not the right comparison. Look at US in the 1960s-70s. What brands came then? What worked then?"

> "I'm very committed to this problem. After 8 years, we've figured out a scalable model where every truck can run on relay using our technology."

> "A very strong entrepreneur in a weak market is a problem. You might be flying the wrong horse for a long time."

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs & Builders

- Don't compete in oversaturated consumer internet; look for real problems in manufacturing and logistics
- Unsexy businesses with real utility can create billions in value
- Understand your country's economic stage before planning; compare to similar-stage economies, not developed ones
- Manufacturing businesses need 3-5 years to gain traction; patience is essential
- Study ground-level problems (spend time with truckers, factory workers) before building solutions
- Infrastructure play matters more than consumer play for India's next 20 years
- Trust VCs and PEs will eventually recognize manufacturing as a massive wealth creation opportunity

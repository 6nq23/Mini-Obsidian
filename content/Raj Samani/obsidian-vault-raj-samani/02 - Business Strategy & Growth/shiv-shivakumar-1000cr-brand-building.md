# Building a ₹1000 Cr Brand: What Every Startup Must Know

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Speed beats size in competition. Success requires understanding your specific customer segment deeply, executing faster than competitors, and thinking big while maintaining execution excellence. Big companies fail because they're slow despite legacy; startups win by moving fast.

## Guest Overview
Shiv Shivakumar is an experienced brand builder and business strategist who has worked with founders across multiple industries. He brings insights from observing what separates billion-rupee brands from failures and how startups can compete against established giants.

## Key Insights

### Speed Beats Size in Competition
- A fast company will always beat a slow company
- Big companies like Tata and Reliance's newer consumer ventures (Big Basket, 1MG, Tata Newer) have failed against startups
- Legacy businesses are living off past reputation but failing to execute in new spaces
- Startups should exploit speed as their primary competitive weapon against larger competitors

### The Importance of Thinking Big vs. Executing Brilliantly
- Reliance (under Mukesh Ambani) excels at two things: thinking at scale AND executing brilliantly
- Most small founders underestimate how big companies think about market opportunity
- Big companies synthesize vast amounts of information to make decisions; small companies lack this capability
- But big companies are slow; startups compensate with velocity

### Customer Segmentation and Understanding
- Indian customers want value for money across all price points
- Chinese consumers prioritize innovation (similar to Japanese consumers)
- American customers have unique purchasing power: full returns, no questions asked
- Understanding these nuances is critical for product-market fit

### The Speed Advantage Over Size
- Small companies should leverage speed to stay ahead
- Speed allows for faster iteration, faster product launches, faster pivot capability
- Scale comes later; speed comes first
- Don't try to outsmart big companies—out-execute them

### Market Evolution and Consumer Expectations
- Success requires identifying what the target customer truly wants (not what you think they want)
- Build for your specific segment, then expand methodically
- Competition in India is intense; winners differentiate through execution velocity

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Speed-Execution Matrix**: Prioritize speed of execution over perfection; fast iteration beats slow perfection
- **Big Company Capability vs. Small Company Speed**: Map your startup's advantages (speed, agility, focus) against incumbent weaknesses (process, bureaucracy)
- **Customer Value Hierarchy**: Understand whether your customer seeks value, innovation, convenience, or status—and build accordingly

## Notable Quotes

> "A fast company has beaten a slow company. Speed is the thing a small company should use against a big company."

> "Big companies are able to think very big about what an opportunity can be. But small companies don't have the same access to information."

> "All their new consumer investments have failed. They're living on past legacy. On the other hand, speed in execution is what newer businesses have."

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs & Builders

- Don't compete on resources or legacy; compete on speed and execution
- Understand your customer segment deeply before scaling to adjacent markets
- Innovate faster than established players can respond
- Build feedback loops to stay ahead of competition
- Scale execution brilliantly once product-market fit is proven
- Study failed ventures from big companies to understand where they lost momentum

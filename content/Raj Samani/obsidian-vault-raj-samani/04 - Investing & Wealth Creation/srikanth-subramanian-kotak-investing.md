# The Art of Investing Money & Wealth Creation

> [!abstract]
> **TL;DR:** Wealth creation is about finding expertise you trust and letting them manage high-stakes decisions. Trust is built through consistent values, not promises. Real wealth comes from compounding over decades, not quick returns. Diversification beats concentration for most investors, but conviction bets beat diversification for those secure enough to take them.

## Guest Overview
Srikanth Subramanian is a senior investment expert at Kotak Mahindra Bank with deep experience in portfolio management, wealth advisory, and financial decision-making. He works with India's wealthiest individuals and understands what separates wealth creators from wealth accumulators.

## Key Insights

### Trust is More Valuable Than Returns
- Wealthy people spend most meeting time assessing: "Can I trust this person?"
- They don't debate strategy, portfolio mix, or fees—those are table stakes
- What matters: Does this person have ethical frameworks? Do they have my interest at heart?
- Trust creates "leverage" because you don't need to micromanage; you can focus on other opportunities

### High-Stakes Decisions Require Expertise
- Treating investing like a DIY project (self-medicating with Google) is risky
- Money decisions are high-stakes; you need someone to hold your hand through volatility
- The expertise gap explains why funds outperform, but investors underperform their funds (timing issues)
- Wealth managers protect you from emotional decisions during market downturns

### The Compounding Game in Every Life Domain
- Compounding extends beyond money: reputation, relationships, skills, trust
- Breaking compounding (switching jobs, dropping projects, changing advisors) resets progress
- A CEO's compounding is leadership (transferable); not domain expertise
- Most wealth is created through 20-30 year compounding, not quick wins

### Building Trust as an Asset
- Trust is a compounding asset, like money
- Building it requires daily decisions that align with stakeholders' interests
- Tata brand has trust from centuries of doing right by consumers and stakeholders
- One breach can crack decades of trust; one consistent act of integrity compounds trust

### Past Performance ≠ Future Performance
- Most portfolio decisions are based on last 1-3 years of performance
- But past performance is included with a disclaimer that almost nobody reads
- When markets shift conditions (interest rates up, economic cycle changes), past winners become losers
- Study what strategy performed in SIMILAR economic conditions, not just recent conditions

### Portfolio Strategy vs. Market Conditions
- A fund manager who excels in low-interest-rate environments may struggle in rising-rate environments
- Instead of chasing past performance, understand the strategy and the conditions it thrives in
- Recency bias causes investors to buy what's already done well (entering at the top)
- Data-driven investors beat emotion-driven investors over long periods

### The Probability and Uncertainty Framework
- Expected value = outcome × probability. Most people chase hope over expected value
- Example: Lottery (promise $1M at 1% chance = $10K expected value); bank investment ($20K guaranteed)
- Humans pick the lottery (hope) over rational expected value
- Wealthy people understand probability and make expected-value decisions

### Concentration vs. Diversification
- Secure people can concentrate bets (one strong story); insecure people need diversification
- Diversification is safer but caps upside; concentration is riskier but enables exponential returns
- Elon Musk concentrated bets on EVs when others diversified across sectors
- For most people, diversification is safer; for convictions, concentration works

## Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Trust-Returns Matrix**: Prioritize finding trustworthy advisors over chasing highest returns
- **Compounding Consciousness Framework**: Every decision either preserves or breaks compounding
- **Strategy-Condition Fit**: Match past-performing strategy to current economic conditions
- **Expected Value Framework**: Train yourself to calculate expected value instead of chasing narrative upside
- **Concentration-Security Spectrum**: Only concentrate bets once you have security

## Lessons for Entrepreneurs & Builders

- When raising capital or building investor relationships, prioritize trust over pitch quality
- Hire wealth managers or financial advisors for high-stakes money decisions
- Understand your own probability bias and expected value calculations
- Build trust as a core asset in every business relationship
- Diversify your portfolio early (security); concentrate bets once secure
- Study economic conditions, not just past returns, when making investment decisions
- Consistency and alignment matter more than performance promises
- Invest in relationships and reputation as much as financial returns
